01 About me

I try to break computing bottlenecks by designing the hardware and the algorithm together, from transistors in a memory array to the sensor on your wrist.

I'm a final-year student of Electrical, Electronic and Communication Engineering at the Military Institute of Science and Technology (MIST), Dhaka, graduating in 2027 with a CGPA of 3.92 out of 4.00.

My research runs from a 6T SRAM compute-in-memory array for binary neural networks, simulated in 45 nm CMOS, to FormSense, a wrist-worn 6-DOF IMU that grades workout form and spots fatigue. I also work on the flight electronics of a hybrid VTOL aircraft.

Industrial attachments at Neural Semiconductor Limited (digital IC design, CMOS layout, memory nonidealities) and Walton Hi-Tech Industries PLC. (SMT/PCB assembly, inspection, quality control) showed me how designs become products.

03 Research

6T SRAM · CIM
IEEE ISVLSIPeer-reviewed conference paper45 nm CMOS

Variation-Aware SRAM Compute-in-Memory Boolean Architecture for Binary Neural Networks

Hasna Hena Jui*, Md. Tanvir Ahmed Tonmoy*, Mahfuzur Rahman Kabbo, Abdullah Al Mahi, Nasiruddin Mahmud Himel, Sunjida Sultana, Twisha Titirsha, Touhidur Rahman · MIST, BRAC University, Neural Semiconductor

A 6T SRAM array that runs Boolean operations inside the memory, removing the memory-wall bottleneck. Monte Carlo simulation links process-induced delay variation to logic errors, and a cross-layer framework shows what those errors do to a binary neural network.

58.27% → 89.34%MNIST accuracy recovered
0.095 → 0.0025XNOR error probability
~3.17 pJper CIM operation
~12.27 µJper inference
Key contributions
  • 6T CMOS SRAM compute-in-memory architecture in 45 nm technology executing Boolean operations within the memory matrix.
  • 1,200-point Monte Carlo simulations of process-induced propagation delay to set timing-induced logic error probabilities.
  • Hardware-aware BNN evaluation for MNIST digit classification across circuit and network layers.
  • Try a simplified version in the CIM lab above.
6-DOF IMU · 100 Hz
IEEE iCACCESS 2026Peer-reviewed conference paperLead author

FormSense: A Continuous Fatigue and Form Tracking Application for Resistance Workout Monitoring Utilizing Phase-grade IMU Analysis

Abdullah Al Mahi, Munawar Arif Nitol, Md. Mehedi Hasan Bhuiyan, Sadman Rahman, Kazi Newaj Faisal* · MIST · 2nd iCACCESS, ULAB, Dhaka

A wrist-worn IMU pipeline that finds faults inside a single repetition, without a motion-capture lab or a large labelled dataset, and warns the user about fatigue before it leads to injury.

3 phasesInitiation, main drive, deceleration
r = 0.20–0.87agreement with expert athletes
Good / Avg / Badform grade per phase
Key contributions
  • 100 Hz sensor data turned into orientation-invariant magnitudes, then each repetition segmented into three biomechanical phases.
  • Form graded with dynamic percentile thresholds mapped against expert athletes.
  • Plain-English feedback that names the flaw, such as acceleration path mismatch or wrist rotation slip, and detects fatigue within a set.

D-shaped PCF-SPR
Under journal reviewFirst authorCOMSOL · FEM

A Highly Sensitive Single-Layer Gold-Coated Symmetric D-Shaped PCF-SPR Sensor for Malaria Stage Screening and Cancer Cell Refractive-Index Sensing

Abdullah Al Mahi, Mahibul Haque, K M Mustafizur Rahman*

A 25-air-hole D-shaped photonic crystal fiber with one flat gold layer. Surface plasmon resonance shifts with the refractive index of the sample, which separates healthy from cancerous cells and tracks the stages of Plasmodium falciparum.

45,000 nm/RIUpeak wavelength sensitivity
658.83 RIU⁻¹amplitude sensitivity
2.22 × 10⁻⁶ RIUresolution
Key results
  • Healthy versus cancerous cell lines discriminated at 32,143 nm/RIU.
  • Malaria progression tracked across ring (50,000 nm/RIU), trophozoite and schizont stages.
  • Designed and analysed with the finite element method in COMSOL Multiphysics.
